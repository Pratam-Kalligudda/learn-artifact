Every trap in the ZeMA hydraulic dataset, in the order you will hit them

A 60-second load cycle, repeated 2,205 times, watched by 17 channels at three different sampling rates, with four faults deliberately superimposed. The four facts that change what you build

If you read nothing else, read these. Each one silently corrupts a result rather than throwing an error, which is why each is discovered late and painfully.

PS4 is dead

Reads exactly 0.000 for 1,238 whole cycles and is flat for 1,492. It only wakes up inside the healthy-cooler block, so PS4 ≠ 0 predicts "cooler healthy" for reasons of wiring history, not physics.

The cooler is blocked by time

The cooler label changes exactly twice, at cycle 732 and cycle 1464, and never goes back. Cycle index alone predicts it. A random train/test split on the cooler task is invalid and will report a number that does not generalise.

There is no "normal"

Only 21 cycles have all four components at optimum, 10 of which are flagged stable. A global healthy baseline cannot be built. Baseline per component instead — those pools run 599 to 1,221 cycles.

1 Hz destroys the valve

The valve fault is a 70–130 ms timing shift. Every cooling and pump signature survives 1 Hz telemetry; the valve signature vanishes completely and leaves no trace that anything was lost.

02 · The physical system

An electro-hydraulic power unit with two circuits and one shared tank

Before any sensor name means anything you need to know what machine is being watched. DOC calls it "a hydraulic test rig [consisting] of a primary working and a secondary cooling-filtration circuit which are connected via the oil tank", which "cyclically repeats constant load cycles (duration 60 seconds)".

INF In equipment terms this is the same class of machine as the power pack of an industrial press, an injection moulder, an excavator arm, or the intensifier stage of a waterjet cutter. Nothing about the analysis is specific to a laboratory — the failure modes represented are the ordinary wear-out modes of industrial hydraulics.

The coupling, stated once so it is never forgotten
  • A cooler fault has no local signature. It shifts bulk oil temperature, so it appears on TS1, TS2, TS3 and TS4 simultaneously — and on PS5/PS6 through viscosity.
  • Oil temperature contaminates the working circuit. Hot oil is thinner and develops less pressure across the same restriction, so a working-circuit pressure reading partly reports the state of the cooler.

Two traps worth stating explicitly

FS1 and FS2 are on different circuits

Despite the adjacent naming: FS1 is the working circuit and gates with the valve — exactly 0 l/min while the valve is shut. FS2 is the cooling circuit and runs continuously at ~9.65 l/min. Getting these the wrong way round is the single easiest mistake to make in this dataset, and it produces results that look entirely plausible.

Exact zeros are meaningful, not missing

PS2 = 0.000 means the valve is shut. FS1 = 0.000 means no flow is commanded. SE = 0.000 means the machine is doing no work. A loader that treats zero as a null, or imputes over it, destroys the valve signal — which is the information the entire cycle structure depends on.

Data types and precision MEAS

All values parse cleanly as IEEE-754 doubles. Observed precision is 2–3 decimal places (160.485, 2495.509, 0.613), consistent with fixed-point instrument output rather than full float precision. No scientific notation, no thousands separators, no quoted fields, no locale-specific decimal commas. Standard tab-delimited parsing works without configuration.

What is not in the dataset

A developer will look for each of these and will not find it:

  • No timestamps. Not a date, not a clock, not an elapsed-time column. Anywhere.
  • No header rows and no column names. Channel identity comes solely from the filename.
  • No index column. Row position is the cycle identifier.
  • No units in the data. Units come from description.txt only.
  • No rig schematic or sensor placement diagram.
  • No engineering limits — no design pressures, alarm setpoints or maintenance thresholds.
  • No machine identifier. All 2,205 cycles come from one rig.
  • No operator log, maintenance record or event history.
  • No test-plan document explaining the order in which conditions were set.

The cooler label is a clock in disguise

The cooler label changes exactly twice across 2,205 cycles, and both changes are monotonic. The technician evidently ran the entire failed-cooler campaign, then the entire degraded campaign, then the entire healthy campaign.

Four consequences, all of which must be designed around
  1. Any model given the cycle index — or any feature that drifts monotonically with it — can score near 100% on the cooler task while learning nothing about cooling.
  2. A random train/test split on the cooler task is invalid. It will report an accuracy that does not generalise.

  4. Cross-block trending is meaningless. A line drawn through oil temperature across the whole dataset shows the test schedule, not machine degradation.

PS5 and PS6 are viscosity gauges, and that makes them valuable

PS5 and PS6 look uninformative at first glance: they vary by only 0.022 bar within a cycle. But between cycles they vary by 0.58 bar, and that variation is almost perfectly anti-correlated with oil temperature.

corr(PS5 cycle-mean, TS1 cycle-mean) = -0.993
PS5 mean by cooler grade:   3% → 8.514 bar    20% → 9.077 bar    100% → 9.890 bar
Measured η² of PS5 against the cooler label: 0.963

INF Mechanism. Hydraulic oil viscosity falls sharply with temperature. The fixed-displacement circulation pump develops less pressure across the same fixed restriction when the oil is thinner. PS5 and PS6 are therefore functioning as indirect oil-viscosity measurements.

Why this matters operationally

They provide a confirmatory path to cooling condition that is physically independent of the thermocouples. A thermocouple failure and a pressure-transducer failure are uncorrelated events, so agreement between them is real evidence — and disagreement is a sensor-fault signal.

Thermal state contaminates the working circuit

The two circuits are coupled through the shared oil tank, so oil temperature influences working-circuit behaviour. The clearest measured example: the healthy-pump flow-per-watt ratio FS1/EPS1 shifts systematically with oil temperature across the three thermal regimes.

Implication

Working-circuit measurements are not thermally neutral. Any comparison of working-circuit behaviour across cycles at different oil temperatures needs a temperature covariate.

Three columns the rig computed, and the formulae recovered by regression

CP — Cooling Power · r² = 0.9992

CP [kW] = 0.026939 × FS2 [l/min] × (TS3 − TS4) [K] + 0.000472

  r²                  = 0.999196
  mean abs residual   = 0.00555 kW
  max abs residual    = 0.05319 kW

The leading coefficient is a physical constant. For hydraulic oil at ρ ≈ 0.87 kg/l and cp ≈ 1.86 kJ/(kg·K):

ρ · cp / 60 = 0.87 × 1.86 / 60 = 0.026970      against a fitted 0.026939

Agreement to 0.1%. This is the standard thermodynamic heat-removal identity Q = ṁ · cp · ΔT, with the ÷60 converting l/min to l/s.

It establishes the cooler topology

The identity only holds with TS3 as the hot inlet and TS4 as the cooled outlet. This is the strongest evidence for the sensor placement, and the only one grounded in physics rather than circumstance.

It contains no new information

CP is fully determined by FS2, TS3 and TS4. Treat it as a convenience channel. Never feed it to a model alongside its own inputs.

Train / test split protocol — mandatory POC

  1. Cooler — three-fold block split. Train on two cooler blocks, test on the held-out third; rotate; report the mean. A random split is invalid.
  2. Valve, pump — labels are balanced across cooler blocks, so a stratified random split is legitimate. Stratify on the joint 4-tuple label.
  3. Accumulator — unbalanced across cooler blocks. Either stratify on the joint label including cooler, or restrict to one block.

Reference replay loop

CYCLE_SECONDS = 60
T0 = datetime(2024, 1, 1, tzinfo=timezone.utc)

def replay(data, profile, speed=60.0, target_rate=10):
    """Yield (timestamp, cycle_id, t_in_cycle, channel, value) tuples."""
    for i in range(2205):
        cycle_start = T0 + timedelta(seconds=i * CYCLE_SECONDS)
        for ch, native in CHANNELS.items():
            if ch == 'PS4':
                continue                       # dead channel
            step = max(1, native // target_rate)
            eff  = native / step
            for j, v in enumerate(data[ch][i, ::step]):
                yield (cycle_start + timedelta(seconds=j / eff),
                       i, j / eff, ch, float(v))
